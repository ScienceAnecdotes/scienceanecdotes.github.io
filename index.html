From submission to upload

We go through submissions at least once a day. If your work already has a reviewer, the process moves faster, since we only need to send them a confirmation email and continue once they reply. If it doesn't have one yet, expect to wait a little longer. We try to give everyone a fair chance to be featured, so submissions are worked through in the order they arrive.

Did not write before? We teach you!

Never written for an audience, or never touched LaTeX before? Join our Discord community and we'll coach you 1 to 1, from formatting your first document to finding your voice on the page. We will provide a tutorial video of how to get going with the templates, how to export your work and send us your work.

Join the team

Want to become part of the program, gain experience, or help other writers finish their work? Join our Discord community and fill in an application there. Reviewers get to talk directly with the founder and take part in shaping what gets published. We read every application, but please be patient, we're working through them in order to give everyone an equal chance.

Connect on social media

Made a short reel about your anecdote or article? Tag our Instagram account and we'll share it on our page too, once your work is already live on the website.

Report or remove work

Spotted something on the site that shouldn't be there? Message us directly on Discord and we'll look into it.

Want your own work taken down instead? Use a removal button below and fill in the same information from your original submission, plus your reason for removing it. Removals are handled by hand and can take up to a full month to complete.

Privacy Policy

Science Anecdotes is run by Ali Hamdan as a free, community-driven space for sharing science and math writing. This page explains what we ask for when you submit a piece of work, what we do with it, and what you can expect from us in return.

What we collect

When you send us an anecdote or article, we ask for thirteen things: your email, your full name, your age, your country, a title, a short description, the background a reader needs before reading it, your Instagram handle, your references, your reviewer's full name, your reviewer's specialty, your reviewer's email, and a short line about your own background. We ask for all of it up front so we're not chasing you for missing pieces later.

What becomes public

Only part of that list ever appears on the website: your name, the title, the short description, the reading background, your Instagram handle, your references, and your reviewer's name and specialty. These are the fields that give your work context and give you credit for it.

What stays with us

Your email, age, country, your reviewer's email, and the short note about your own background are never published. We keep them so we can reach you about your submission, confirm details with your reviewer, and, if you're open to it, invite you to future opportunities on the page. We don't sell or hand this information to anyone outside the team running Science Anecdotes.

Likes and Google sign in

You can like a work by pressing the heart. At that moment we ask you to sign in with Google, so that each account can like a work only once. Reading the site never needs an account. Signing in shares your email and a unique account ID with Google Firebase, the service that stores likes for us. Each like we store holds only the work's ID and an anonymous account ID, so no like is shown next to your name or email, and only the total is public. The sign in service keeps the account's email in a user list that only the team can see, and we never sell or share it.

How long we keep it

We hold on to submission details for as long as your work stays on the site, plus a reasonable window afterward in case a removal or correction comes up. If you'd like your information deleted sooner, email us and we'll take care of it.

If you're a minor

We ask for age so we can pace how we talk to authors, not to exclude anyone. If you're not yet an adult where you live, we'd encourage you to loop in a parent or guardian before you submit, especially since your name and Instagram handle will be visible publicly.

Your say in this

You can ask us at any time what we have on file for you, ask us to fix it, or ask us to delete it, minus whatever we need to keep to process an active removal. Reach us at science.anecdotes.lb@gmail.com for any of this.

Terms of Use

By sending us your work, you're agreeing to the following.

It's still your work

Submitting to Science Anecdotes doesn't sign your work away. You keep ownership of what you write. What you're giving us is permission to host it, format it for the site, and share it, on the website, on our own Instagram and WhatsApp, and in anything we post to promote the page.

What we expect from a submission

Your work should be your own, honestly sourced, and accurately attributed. Naming a reviewer is your confirmation that they've agreed to have their name, specialty, and email passed along to us for that role. Anything plagiarized, hateful, knowingly false, or built around someone else's private information without consent will be turned down or taken down, no exceptions.

Editorial say

We read everything before it goes up. We can ask for edits, reject a submission, or take something down later if it stops meeting our standards, and we won't always be able to explain every call in detail. This isn't personal, it's how we keep the page worth reading.

Removals

If you want your own work taken off the site, use the removal buttons on the Share Your Work page. Because we process these by hand, a removal can take up to a full month from when we receive your request.

No guarantees

Everything on Science Anecdotes is shared for learning and curiosity. Reviewers help us catch mistakes, but neither Ali Hamdan nor Science Anecdotes can promise that every fact on the site is airtight. Read critically, and let us know if something looks wrong.

Getting in touch

Questions about either of these policies, or a request to fix or remove your information, can go to science.anecdotes.lb@gmail.com.
